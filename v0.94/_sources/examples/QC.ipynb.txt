    "# Q vs Conditional Forecasting\n",
    "\n",
    "There are two different approaches to forecasting the priceable (i.e. price sensitive) demand for\n",
    "unrestricted networks: Q forecasting, and conditional forecasting.\n",
    "\n",
    "Under Q forecasting, each priceable booking is converted into an equivalent number of Q-class\n",
    "bookings, where the \"Q\" class is the lowest possible fare class for the relevant path. Forecasts are\n",
    "then made for how much demand there would be for that Q class, and then those forecasts are\n",
    "broadcast up to the various higher fare classes, based on the sellup probability in each time frame\n",
    "along the booking curve.\n",
    "\n",
    "Under conditional forecasting, bookings are all recorded in the class where they occur, and the\n",
    "level of demand in that class is computed *conditional* on it being the lowest available fare class\n",
    "at that time.\n",
    "\n",
    "These two methodologies are similar, and indeed with certain configurations and under the right\n",
    "conditions (e.g. flights never sell out, the lowest available fare class is always the same for full\n",
    "time frames) the methodologies become identical. In practice, and in most simulations, these\n",
    "particular conditions do not actually hold, so these algorithms are not actually identical, so\n",
    "PassengerSim allows you to choose one or the other as appropriate."
   ]

    "## Changing RM System Options\n",
    "\n",
    "We can also change some of the options associated with the RM systems. For example, the Q system\n",

    "results, as priceable demand is presumed to be censored and in need of detruncation only when a leg\n",
    "is completely sold out. To remove the detruncation of priceable demand from the Q system, we can use\n",
